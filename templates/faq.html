How this works

The honest, specific version — not just "it's anonymous, trust us."

What does "anonymous" actually mean here?

When you send feedback, the only things ever saved are who it's for, its category, the reworded text, and roughly when to deliver it. Nothing about who wrote it is saved — not your name, not your account, not even a hidden ID linking back to you.

Could an admin, or anyone with access to the server, figure out who sent something?

Not from the database — there's nothing in it to find. The one real limit: while you're actively writing and reviewing a draft, your own browser session is, obviously, yours — that's unavoidable since you're the one typing. The moment you approve and send it, that connection is gone for good, including from your own account.

Why does it take a few hours to show up, instead of right away?

If feedback appeared the instant you sent it, timing alone could give you away ("this showed up right after our meeting..."). Approved feedback is held and delivered in batches at set times instead, so arrival time doesn't track submission time.

Is there a record of what I've sent?

No — on purpose. Keeping a history for you would mean storing the exact link this whole design exists to avoid. Once you approve something, it's out of your hands entirely, including the record of having sent it.

What happens to what I write before I send it?

It gets reworded to remove identifying language and personal tone before you see it, and you get to compare the original and the reworded version side by side. You can regenerate it, edit it by hand, or discard it entirely — nothing is sent until you approve it.

What if I receive something upsetting?

Feedback here is meant to be constructive, not a place for personal attacks. If something crosses that line, say so to whoever runs this for your team — they can't trace it back to a sender, but they can decide how to handle it going forward.
